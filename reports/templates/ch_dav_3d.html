Footprint from the ICOS data

  • The ICOS footprint model (Kljun et al., 2015) places half of the daytime flux within about 40 to 50 m of the tower, 80 % within about 110 to 120 m and 90 % within about 190 to 210 m.
  • For northerly wind, the lake starts 350 m from the tower along the wind direction. The model assigns on average about 3 % of the flux to the lake. Within the northern sector, closure does not change with this share (EBR 0.42 to 0.47).
  • At the same u*, closure decreases as the footprint gets longer: at u* 0.6 to 0.8 m s⁻¹, EBR is 0.65 when 80 % of the flux comes from within 90 m and 0.37 when it comes from beyond 130 m. In this model the footprint length follows the atmospheric stability, so closure is best in strongly unstable conditions and poorest near neutral.
  • The lake is therefore unlikely to affect the measured fluxes directly through the footprint. It can still affect them indirectly, through cool air and the along-valley flow that crosses it before reaching the forest.
  • The footprint model assumes flat, uniform terrain. On the 16° slope, with the lake and the valley floor upwind, the real source area can differ.
